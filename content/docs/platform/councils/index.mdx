# Councils

A **council** is a structured group of agents. Use one when a problem benefits from multiple perspectives, decomposition, adversarial review, voting, or final synthesis.

Do not use a council when one strong agent plus one or two abilities would be simpler. Councils add cost and coordination overhead, so each member should have a distinct reason to exist.

## What a council contains

| Part | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| Leader | Agent that coordinates the work and usually owns synthesis |
| Members | Additional agents with distinct roles or lenses |
| Delegation strategy | How the leader distributes work |
| Member priority | Ordering or weighting hints when strategy needs them |
| Description | Human-readable reason the council exists |

## Delegation strategies

| Strategy | Best when |
|----------|-----------|
| Dynamic | The leader should decide who to involve at runtime |
| Broadcast | All members should inspect the same question independently |
| Decompose | The work naturally splits into distinct subtasks |
| Round robin | Sequential refinement is useful |
| Vote | The council must choose between clear alternatives |

## Common uses

- Architecture review with implementation, security, and operations perspectives
- Policy review where legal, compliance, and product lenses matter
- Risk assessment with independent specialist outputs
- Strategic planning where the leader decomposes and synthesizes
- Adversarial review where one member proposes and another critiques

## Councils in routines

Councils are most useful as routine steps. The routine decides when the council runs and where the result goes next. The council decides how members contribute and how the leader synthesizes the result.

For simple sequential work, use routine agent steps and gates. For independent judgment or multi-perspective review, use a council step.

## Design checklist

Before creating a council, define:

- The decision or artifact the council should improve
- The leader and final synthesis owner
- Each member's distinct perspective
- The delegation strategy
- The expected member output shape
- How disagreement should be handled
